Finding facts in the fantastic

Some branches of physics hint that our universe is just one of many in a vast “multiverse.”

The main problem with real-world lightsabers is that they would pass through each other. This means no intergalactic duels between Jedis and Siths.

From the shocking powers of electric eels to laser-guided lightning, aiming electricity is more real than it sounds.

Fiction is full of characters with the power to vanish. But some animals have real-life ways to become nearly invisible.

Levitation may seem like fantasy. But all it takes is a little physics — and sound waves, magnetism or electricity.

Linguists can choose to follow, mix or break the rules of real-world languages to create interesting fictional ones.

Simple chemistry could give the reindeer his iconic red snout. But physics would make it look different colors to anyone who spied Rudolph from the ground.

The atoms that make us up couldn’t be shrunk or smashed closer together — at least, not without catastrophic consequences.

Vampires often have human bodies. To survive on blood, they’d need to shed millions of years of evolution.

Mars would need an atmosphere thick enough to hold heat and with enough oxygen for people to breathe. This is how we might terraform Mars.

Although our sun is a dwarf yellow star, it’s more than massive enough to weather any attempts to alter it — super or otherwise.

In fantasy, trees can walk, climb and even fight. Real trees move, too. It just happens in extreme slow mo.
